<div class="hx:mt-6"></div>

Everdisk turns your iPhone or iPad into a wireless drive that shares files, photos, videos and music with any device on your local network — a smart TV, a Mac or PC, a web browser, or another phone — with no accounts, no cloud, and nothing to install on the other side. This FAQ answers the questions people ask most and walks through common real-world scenarios. For step-by-step instructions, see the [Everdisk User Guide](/docs/guide/everdisk/).

<div class="hx:w-full">

{{% details title="What is Everdisk?" closed="true" %}}
Everdisk is a **wireless drive** app for iPhone and iPad. It turns your device into a hub that shares the files, photos, videos and music you choose with anything on your local network, and it also connects to other servers so you can browse and stream their files.<br><br>

It runs four servers at once — **DLNA** for TVs, **HTTP** for web browsers, **WebDAV** for Finder/Windows/Linux, and **FTP** for file apps — so every device connects the way it likes. There are no accounts and no cloud: you tap **Start** and you are ready. It also includes a file manager, a media player, a photo gallery, document scanning to PDF, and zip/unzip.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="How does Everdisk work?" closed="true" %}}
When you tap **Start**, Everdisk turns your device into a server on your local network and shares the content you selected. Four different connection methods run at the same time so different devices can connect in the way they prefer:<br><br>

- **DLNA** — smart TVs and media players find your device automatically and show your media with thumbnails.<br>
- **HTTP** — anyone opens a link in a web browser to browse and download your files.<br>
- **WebDAV** — your device appears as a normal network drive in Finder, Windows or Linux.<br>
- **FTP** — file apps and power tools connect over FTP.<br><br>

Everything happens over your local Wi-Fi (or a USB cable to a Mac) and never touches the internet.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="Do I need an account, a login, or the internet?" closed="true" %}}
No. Everdisk needs no account and no internet connection. It works entirely on your **local network**, or over a **USB cable** to a Mac. Nothing is uploaded to any cloud. The only optional login is one **you** can set to password-protect your own sharing.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="How do I start sharing?" closed="true" %}}
Open the **Sharing** tab, tap **What to Share** and choose your files, photos and/or music, then tap the big **Start** button. The **How to Connect** section then shows the exact addresses other devices use to connect. Tap **Stop** to take everything offline again.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="What can I share?" closed="true" %}}
Three kinds of content, in any combination:<br><br>

- **Files and folders** — the app's Documents folder (shared by default), plus any folders or individual files you add with **Add Folder** and **Add File**.<br>
- **Photos and videos** — your whole Photos library with **Allow access to all Photos Library**, or only selected items with **Add Photos**.<br>
- **Music** — your whole Music library with **Allow access to all Music Library**, or selected songs with **Add Tracks**.<br><br>

You must select at least one thing before sharing can start. Protected (DRM) and cloud-only tracks can't be shared.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="How do I share photos with my computer?" closed="true" %}}
1. On the **Sharing** screen, open **What to Share → Photos and Videos** and turn on **Allow access to all Photos Library** (or add specific photos).<br>
2. Tap **Start**.<br>
3. On the computer, either open the **Browser** address in any web browser and download, or connect to the **Computer (WebDAV)** address from Finder (Mac) or File Explorer (Windows) and drag the photos across.<br><br>

Tip: keep the Photos **Format** on **Original** for the fastest transfer.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="How do I watch my videos or photos on a TV?" closed="true" %}}
1. In **Settings → Sharing → Connections**, make sure **TV & Media Center (DLNA)** is on.<br>
2. Tap **Start**.<br>
3. On the TV, open its built-in media player or media-server app and pick your device by its name from the list of media servers.<br>
4. Browse and play. Preview thumbnails appear automatically.<br><br>

If an older TV won't play a video, lower the quality in **Settings → Sharing → Videos** so Everdisk converts it to a more compatible format.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="How do I listen to my music?" closed="true" %}}
Turn on **Allow access to all Music Library** (or add tracks) under **What to Share**, then **Start**. You can then play your music from another device's web browser (there is a built-in player with a queue, shuffle and repeat), from a DLNA TV, or in Everdisk itself using the **Documents → Music** mode. Music is always shared in its original quality.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="How do I watch films inside Everdisk?" closed="true" %}}
Tap any video — in your Documents, a connected folder, or a server on the Devices tab — and it plays **full screen** using the built-in system player, with standard controls including **AirPlay**, **Picture in Picture** and **playback speed**. Audio plays in a **mini player** that stays at the bottom of the app with a queue, shuffle and repeat.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="How do I open my files in a web browser?" closed="true" %}}
1. In **Settings → Sharing → Connections**, make sure **Browser (HTTP)** is on.<br>
2. Tap **Start** and copy the **Browser** address (or show its QR code).<br>
3. On the other device, open any web browser and type that address.<br><br>

The other person can browse in a grid or list, view photos in a full-screen gallery, play music, and download files — including downloading a whole folder as one **Archive.zip**. If you turned on **Files Editing**, they can also upload files back to you. Nothing needs to be installed on their side.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="How do I use my device as a network drive in Finder?" closed="true" %}}
1. In **Settings → Sharing → Connections**, make sure **Computer (WebDAV)** is on, then **Start**.<br>
2. On the Mac, open Finder and choose **Go → Connect to Server** (or press **⌘K**).<br>
3. Enter the **Computer (WebDAV)** address shown in Everdisk and click **Connect**.<br>
4. Enter your login and password if you set one; otherwise connect as a guest.<br><br>

Your device mounts like any network drive. With **Files Editing** on you can drag files both ways; with it off the drive is read-only.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="How do I connect from Windows or Linux?" closed="true" %}}
Use the **Computer (WebDAV)** address.<br><br>

- **Windows:** open File Explorer → right-click **This PC** → **Add a network location** (or map a network drive), and enter the WebDAV address.<br>
- **Linux:** in your file manager choose **Connect to Server** and enter the WebDAV address (for example with a `davs://` or `dav://` prefix).<br><br>

Enter the login and password if you set one.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="Does Everdisk work without Wi-Fi?" closed="true" %}}
Yes, with a **Mac** and a cable. Plug your iPhone or iPad into the Mac, tap **Trust** if asked, and tap **Start**. A **Cable Connection** address ending in `.local` appears on the Sharing screen; connect to it from Finder (**⌘K**). This works with no Wi-Fi at all — great on a plane, in a hotel, or on a locked network — and it is faster and more private than Wi-Fi.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="Can I use the USB cable with a Windows PC or Android?" closed="true" %}}
No. The cable connection works with a **Mac** only. Windows PCs and Android devices connect over **Wi-Fi**. (On Windows you can still drag files into the Everdisk folder using the Apple Devices app or iTunes over the cable, which is separate from live sharing.)
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="Why do I need to keep the app open while sharing?" closed="true" %}}
Your iPhone or iPad is the server, and iOS does not let apps keep a network server running in the background. So sharing works only while Everdisk is **open on screen**. For long transfers, keep the app in the foreground, plug into power, and set **Auto-Lock** to **Never** in the iOS Settings app. You can also turn on **Notify before disconnecting** so Everdisk reminds you before the system pauses it.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="Which is faster — Wi-Fi or the cable?" closed="true" %}}
The **USB cable to a Mac** is faster and more reliable than Wi-Fi, because the data goes straight over the cable. It is also the most private option, since nothing passes through your router or the internet. Use it for large photo and video transfers.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="What is the difference between the four servers (DLNA, HTTP, WebDAV, FTP)?" closed="true" %}}
They all share the same content but suit different devices:<br><br>

- **TV & Media Center (DLNA)** — for smart TVs and media players.<br>
- **Browser (HTTP)** — for any web browser, no install needed.<br>
- **Computer (WebDAV)** — makes your device a network drive in Finder/Windows/Linux.<br>
- **Other Apps & Devices (FTP)** — for file apps and power tools.<br><br>

You can turn any of them on or off in **Settings → Sharing → Connections**.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="How do I password-protect my files?" closed="true" %}}
Go to **Settings → Sharing → Access** and set a **Login** and **Password**. The Browser, Computer and Other Apps connections will then ask for those details. Leave both empty for open access.<br><br>

Note: the **DLNA** (TV) connection can't be password protected, so it is open to anyone on the same Wi-Fi while it is on. Turn it off if you only want protected connections.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="Can I stop people from changing my files?" closed="true" %}}
Yes. The **Files Editing** toggle in **Settings → Sharing → Access** controls this. With it **on** (the default), connected devices can upload, rename and delete. Turn it **off** to make your shared files **read-only** — others can view and download but not change anything.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="How do I block a device I don't recognize?" closed="true" %}}
On the **Sharing** screen, find the device under **Who is Connected**, tap its more-actions button, and choose **Block this device**. Manage blocked devices in **Settings → Sharing → Access → Blocked Devices**, where you can unblock one or **Unblock All**.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="If someone deletes a shared file, is it gone forever?" closed="true" %}}
Not by default. Deleted files go to a recoverable trash so you can get them back. If you prefer immediate deletion with no recovery, turn on **Permanently Delete Files** in **Settings → File Manager → Deleting Files**.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="Is Everdisk private and safe?" closed="true" %}}
Yes. Everdisk shares only over your **local network** — nothing goes to the internet and there is no cloud account. For the best privacy, share on a home or private Wi-Fi you trust, add a login and password, and be careful on public Wi-Fi. The **USB cable to a Mac** is the most private option of all. Everdisk needs the iOS **Local Network** permission so nearby devices can find it.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="Can Everdisk connect to my NAS or other servers?" closed="true" %}}
Yes. The **Devices** tab connects **to** other servers on your network. Tap **New Connection** and choose **DLNA / UPnP**, **WebDAV**, **FTP** or **SFTP**, enter the address and any login, and you can browse, stream and download. On WebDAV, FTP and SFTP servers you can also create folders, upload, rename, move and delete. DLNA servers are read-only.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="Can I connect to cloud storage like Google Drive or Dropbox?" closed="true" %}}
Everdisk does not sign in to cloud accounts such as iCloud Drive, Google Drive, Dropbox or OneDrive. It connects only to **local-network and directly-addressed** servers (DLNA, WebDAV, FTP, SFTP). A cloud service is reachable only if it offers a **WebDAV** address you can type into a new WebDAV connection.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="What is the Devices tab for?" closed="true" %}}
It is the client side of Everdisk: **Connections** lists servers you saved, and **Available Devices** lists servers found automatically on your network. Tap one to browse, stream or download its files, or tap **New Connection** to add a DLNA, WebDAV, FTP or SFTP server by hand.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="How do I download files from a server to my device?" closed="true" %}}
Open the server on the **Devices** tab, browse to what you want, and choose **Download** (select several items for a batch). Downloads run in the background, appear in **File Transfers** (top-left of the Documents tab), and land in your **Documents** folder.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="Does Everdisk have a file manager?" closed="true" %}}
Yes. The **Documents** tab is a full file manager. You can browse in list or grid, create folders, rename, move, copy, upload and delete, and open the **Info** screen for any file. Downloads land here, and the Documents folder is shared by default.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="How do I zip (archive) files?" closed="true" %}}
In the **Documents** tab, select one or more items and choose **Archive**. A single item is zipped on its own; several items are bundled into one **Archive.zip**. In a web browser you can also download a whole folder or several selected items as a single zip.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="How do I unzip an archive?" closed="true" %}}
Tap a **.zip** or **.rar** file in the **Documents** tab and Everdisk extracts it into the current folder, with a progress bar you can cancel.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="How do I scan a document to PDF?" closed="true" %}}
In the **Documents** tab, open the more-actions (•••) menu and choose **Scan Documents**. Point the camera at each page — Everdisk finds the edges and straightens the page automatically — and all the pages are saved together as one **multi-page PDF** in the current folder. You need a device with a camera.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="What PDF tools does Everdisk include?" closed="true" %}}
Everdisk has a built-in **PDF Tools** suite. Open any PDF's **•••** menu (or its Info screen) in the **Documents** tab and tap **PDF Tools**. It is grouped into three areas:<br><br>

**Optimize**<br>
- **Compress** — make a PDF smaller. You see the current size and an estimated result size for each option before you save.<br>
- **Text Recognition (OCR)** — turn a scanned PDF into a **searchable PDF**, or export just the text to a **.txt** file.<br>
- **Flatten** — burn annotations and form entries into the page so they can no longer be edited.<br><br>

**Security**<br>
- **Password** — set, change or remove the password required to open the PDF.<br>
- **Permissions** — review what the PDF allows (copying, printing, commenting, and more).<br><br>

**Metadata**<br>
- **Edit Metadata** — change or clear the title, author, subject, keywords and other document info.<br><br>

PDF Tools are included for free. If a PDF is password-protected, Everdisk asks for its password before you can use the tools.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="How do I compress a PDF?" closed="true" %}}
Open the PDF's **•••** menu → **PDF Tools** → **Compress**. Everdisk shows the current file size and an estimated size for each option:<br><br>

- **Standard** keeps the text selectable and only re-compresses the images.<br>
- **High**, **Medium**, **Small** and **Smallest** re-render the pages as images at a lower resolution — best for scans and photos, but the text is no longer selectable.<br><br>

Tap the option you want and Everdisk saves the smaller PDF.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="How do I make a scanned PDF searchable (OCR)?" closed="true" %}}
Open the PDF's **•••** menu → **PDF Tools** → **Text Recognition**. Choose **Searchable PDF** to add a hidden text layer so you can search and select the text (the pages look exactly the same), or **Text File (.txt)** to save just the recognized text next to the PDF. Large or scanned files can take a little while.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="How do I password-protect a PDF file?" closed="true" %}}
Open the PDF's **•••** menu → **PDF Tools** → **Password**. If the PDF isn't protected yet, enter a new password and confirm it to set it. If it is already protected, enter the current password to **remove** it or **change** it. Keep the password somewhere safe — a protected PDF can't be opened without it.<br><br>

This protects the PDF **file** itself, which is different from the **login and password** that protect your whole sharing session (see the security questions above).
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="Can I draw on or sign a PDF in Everdisk?" closed="true" %}}
Yes. Tap a PDF (or a photo) in the **Documents** tab to open it in the built-in viewer, then use the **Markup** tools to **draw, highlight, add text and shapes, and sign** with your finger. Your changes are saved straight back to the file.<br><br>

If you want to make those marks permanent so they can't be edited later, open the PDF's **••• → PDF Tools → Flatten**. To add a password, run OCR, compress, or edit the document info, use the other **PDF Tools** (see the question above).
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="Can I connect a folder that is somewhere else on my device?" closed="true" %}}
Yes. In the **Documents** tab, open the ••• menu and choose **Connect Folder**, then pick any folder on your device. It appears at the top with a **Connected** label and can be shared over the network like any other folder. Use **Disconnect** from its Info to unlink it — the folder and files stay on your device.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="Can people upload files back to my device?" closed="true" %}}
Yes, when **Files Editing** is on. In a web browser they get an **Upload** panel; on a WebDAV or FTP connection they can copy files onto your device. Turn **Files Editing** off (in Settings → Sharing → Access) to make sharing read-only.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="Why does a photo or video transfer slowly?" closed="true" %}}
If the Photos or Videos **Format** is set to **Most Compatible** or the **Quality** is below **Original**, Everdisk converts each file as it is shared, which is slower and uses more battery. Set **Format** and **Quality** to **Original** in **Settings → Sharing** for the fastest transfers, and only switch to Most Compatible if a specific TV or browser can't open a file.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="A device doesn't appear or won't connect. What can I check?" closed="true" %}}
- Make sure both devices are on the **same Wi-Fi network** (or use the Mac cable).<br>
- Confirm sharing is **started** and the right server is **on** in Settings → Sharing → Connections.<br>
- Type the address exactly as shown on the Sharing screen; over Wi-Fi use the shown IP, and over the cable use the **`.local`** name (never `localhost`).<br>
- If you set a login and password, enter them on the other device.<br>
- Check the **Local Network** permission is allowed for Everdisk in the iOS Settings app.<br>
- Make sure the other device isn't in **Blocked Devices**.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="Can I change the name my device shows on the network?" closed="true" %}}
Everdisk gives your device a friendly name and avatar automatically, and you can **Regenerate** them for free. Setting a **custom** name, icon, gradient or photo avatar is part of the Premium **Device Customization** feature.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="Can I change the connection ports?" closed="true" %}}
The defaults are **HTTP 80**, **WebDAV 8080** and **FTP 2121** (DLNA picks its port automatically). You can view them for free, and setting **custom ports** is a Premium feature (**Advanced** in Settings → Sharing).
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="Is Everdisk free, and what does Premium add?" closed="true" %}}
Everdisk is a **free** download. You can run all four servers, protect access with a password, share your whole Photos and Music library, use the file manager, scan to PDF, and connect to other servers. The free version shares up to **5 folders** and saves up to **10 server connections**.<br><br>

A one-time **Premium Lifetime** purchase (not a subscription) adds: **Unlimited Folders**, **Unlimited Connections**, **Photo & Video Conversion** (quality other than Original), **Custom Ports**, **Auto-Start Sharing**, and **Device Customization**.
{{% /details %}}

{{% details title="Is Premium a subscription? Does it work on my other devices?" closed="true" %}}
Premium is a **one-time purchase**, not a subscription — you pay once and it never renews. It supports **Family Sharing** and is shared between your **iPhone, iPad and Mac** through iCloud. Use **Restore Purchases** if you already bought it.
{{% /details %}}
